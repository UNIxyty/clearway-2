01 · ORIENTATION

The Digital Wall at a glance

The Digital Wall is your shared operational picture, split across two surfaces. Both read from the same live Leon feed, but you use them very differently.

SURFACE A · VIEW-ONLY

The wall (display / timeline)

The big dark screen in the ops room. A live timeline of every aircraft against the clock, with a status legend and manual limitations down the side. Nobody edits it — it reflects what the console decides.

Who uses it: everyone on shift, at a glance, from across the room.
SURFACE B · CONTROL

The console

The light-themed app you sign in to. Pick which flight is on the wall, run the NOTAM check, write limitations, manage aircraft and operators, tune settings, and request documents.

Who uses it: the dispatcher / ops user driving the shift.

02 · THE WALL

Reading the timeline

Each aircraft has its own horizontal lane. Flights appear as pills placed against the hour grid — left edge at departure, right edge at arrival. The vertical now-line marks the current minute and is drawn in white (it used to be blue, which clashed with airborne pills). Everything you need to read a flight sits on one line: circles, callsign, pill, markers — with the route and times directly below.

What reaches the wall. A flight is drawn while its departure→arrival interval overlaps the visible window set in Settings (§11). Cancelled, crew-positioning, simulator and superseded flights never appear — the full list is in §6.

Anatomy of a flight row

Left to right: the limitation circles, the callsign, the pill, and the marker row. The route and times sit below the pill and are never truncated. ICAOs are drawn inside the pill body only when they fit — if they don't fit, they aren't drawn inside at all; the line below is always the readable source of truth.

The delay model — planned vs flownUPDATED

This is the biggest change on the wall. The striped hatch now leads the pill: it spans from the scheduled departure (STD) to the actual take-off (T/O). The coloured body is the real flight — from where it actually took off to where it actually landed (or is estimated to land). Read it as: the stripes are the flight you planned; the colour is the flight you flew.

Below the pill, each end shows the actual time plus the signed difference against schedule — late in amber with a +, early in green with a −.

Pill fill = state · solid vs hollow = how much the wall knows

The fill colour tells you where the flight is in its life. The fill style tells you how much to trust it: a solid pill is confirmed by real flight-watch data from Leon; a hollow (outlined) pill is estimated — Leon has no flight-watch data for that leg, so the wall infers the state purely from the schedule. Solid is a fact. Hollow is a presumption — the wall never dresses one up as the other.

Which times show

The wall applies four fixed rules to decide what appears under a pill:

1
T/O overrides everything. The moment a real take-off time exists, it replaces every departure estimate on the row.
2
Before T/O, CTOT and ETD are equal priority — the later one shows. Whichever is further in the future is the honest departure estimate, so that's the one on the wall.
3
BLOFF never displays. Block-off is noise at wall distance; the wall works in take-offs and landings.
4
Airborne shows T/O + ETA. Arrived shows T/O + LDG. Once down, the row is a complete factual record of the leg.

The marker row & weather colouringUPDATED

Three markers can sit beside a flight: ! for a matching Important entry, NTM for an unreviewed NOTAM at one of its airports, and the teal CAA for a matching authority record. Weather lost its chip: the airport ICAO itself is coloured by its flight category, which frees marker space and puts the warning exactly where you're already looking.

The MVT flash — no movement messageNEW

If no T/O has arrived 15 minutes after the expected departure — the CTOT or ETD when one exists, otherwise the STD — the pill's contour starts flashing red. It stops the instant a T/O comes in, and it never fires for arrived or cancelled flights. Both the threshold and the blink rate are configurable in Settings (§11).

03 · THE WALL

The retractable info tab

To see why a flight is flagged, click its pill. A detail card expands in place, directly beneath the flight — the aircraft's row simply grows taller and pushes the rows below down. An open tab never covers other flights; the rest of the timeline stays fully readable.

Tabs are collapsed by default and an open tab auto-collapses after 3 minutes, so a forgotten click can't clutter the wall.

How acknowledgement works

This flight only
Checking clears the indicator for that flight alone. Other flights matching the same entry keep flagging until someone checks them too — a warning read for one leg is not read for all.
Who and when
Every check records the user and the time, so any acknowledgement can be traced.
Daily reset
Acknowledgements reset with the daily check cycle — yesterday's sign-off doesn't cover today's operation.
Circles never vanish
A checked limitation's numbered circle stays on the timeline — it flips from solid to outlined with a dimmed number. Ops still need to know the flight has limitations; checking only says they've been read.

04 · THE WALL

The Upcoming Flight Table

A dockable panel — pin it to the left or right edge of the wall — listing upcoming flights from today 00:01 UTC onwards. It is independent of the timeline's visible window: a flight too far ahead to be drawn on the grid can still sit in the table, and this morning's early legs stay listed after the timeline has moved on.

The colour coding is the point of the table: without opening anything, you can see which airports still need slots or handling, and which flights still have OPS work outstanding.

06 · THE WALL

What the wall does — and doesn't — show

If a flight you expect isn't on the wall, check this list before suspecting a fault. The wall filters deliberately, so the room only sees legs that matter operationally.

NEVER SHOWN
  • Cancelled flights
  • Crew-positioning legs
  • Simulator sessions
  • Superseded / inactive flights (replaced by a newer version of the leg)
  • Flights entirely outside the visibility window (§11)
ALWAYS SHOWN
  • Ferry legs — an empty aircraft still occupies its lane, its slot and its crew.
Estimated states appear only where Leon has no flight-watch data for the leg — and they are always drawn hollow (§2), never as solid fact.

07 · THE WALL

The flight side-overlay

When a dispatcher sends a flight to the wall from the console, a detail overlay slides in from the right. It's the full picture for the one flight that's live — and, like the rest of the wall, it's view-only.

You don't open it here — a dispatcher opens it from the console's Flights page with Show on wall. The banner names who opened it.

Overlay vs info tab. The retractable info tab (§3) is opened at the wall itself by clicking any pill, and carries per-section Checked buttons. The side-overlay is pushed from the console for one flight the whole room should focus on — it has no buttons at all.
1
Header — the callsign, who opened it, and the trip reference.
2
Route & timings — ADEP → ADES with STD/ETD/ATD and STA/ETA/ATA, plus dep/arr delay.
3
Weather — decoded conditions for both airports, each tagged with its category (see §9).
4
Important (IMP) — the standing limitations this flight matches, in full.
5
Limitations — any manual limitation cards that match, further down the panel.
Reading, not doing. The overlay is there to be read from across the room. To change what's shown — swap the flight or close the overlay — go to the console's Flights page.

08 · DAILY WORKFLOW

The daily NOTAM check

Every morning the system runs a NOTAM scan at 10:00 Europe/Riga across every airport on today's flights, and the team reviews the result. Until every airport is signed off, the wall shows a red warning; once they're all done, it flips green.

1
Open NOTAM Check in the console's left nav.
2
Read each airport's flagged NOTAMs, using the keyword colours as a guide.
3
Press CHECKED per airport — your name and time are recorded.
4
When all airports are checked, the wall sign turns green.

09 · REFERENCE

Weather (WX)

Each airport carries a flight category — a single colour-coded read of how good the conditions are. Since the v1.3 redesign there is no separate WX chip on the wall: the category colours the airport's ICAO code itself below the pill (§2), fills the sidebar's WX agenda (§5) and the WX dots in the Upcoming Flight Table (§4), and is decoded in full in the info tab (§3) and the side-overlay (§7).

Weather on the wall is acknowledgment-only — it's there to inform, not something you action or clear. It's checked once a day, alongside the morning NOTAM run, for that day's flights only, giving each airport a departure and arrival category. There's no separate weather page, and it sends no emails.

VFR
Good — visual conditions. Green on the wall.
MVFR
Marginal — reduced ceiling / vis. Amber on the wall.
IFR
Instrument conditions. Drawn uncoloured — the wall's default text colour.
LIFR
Low IFR — the worst category. Red on the wall.

10 · THE CONSOLE

Managing the console

Beyond Flights and NOTAM Check, several pages let you shape what the wall shows — this section covers Operators, Aircraft, Limitations and Important; Settings & colours (§11), Webhooks (§12), Reports (§13) and CAA details (§14) each have a section of their own. Every page carries its own "How this page works" panel at the top. The console uses the full screen width, every flight row on the Flights page shows that flight's timeline markers inline, and the account dropdown (top right) also takes you across to the AIP Data Portal.

Operators

The Leon operators feeding the wall. Watch sync health at a glance, toggle an operator active/inactive, add a new one, or Force sync if the feed looks stale. Every operator is fully editable — name, code, credentials — and if you rotate a refresh token, re-register that operator's webhooks afterwards (§12) or live updates stop.

1
Sync health — data source, last run, cached flights, poll interval, last error.
2
Per-operator row — prefix, last sync, status, and an Active toggle.
3
Add operator — register a new Leon feed.

Aircraft

Choose which aircraft appear on the wall. Hiding an aircraft removes its lane from the timeline immediately. Search, filter by operator, or use Show all / Hide all.

1
On-wall count — how many aircraft are visible vs hidden, with bulk controls.
2
Upcoming counts — flights in the next 7 days and the next departure.
3
Show on wall — per-aircraft toggle; the lane appears or disappears at once.

Limitations REWORKED

Write the manual text limitations shown on the wall sidebar. Set the text, how it matches (flight, airport, country, or any mix — OR semantics), and its schedule. NOTAM, weather and IMP markers are generated automatically elsewhere.

1
Match type — Flight, Airport, Country or Mixed; matching is OR across every target you add. Country and airport pickers draw from the full airports directory.
2
Date window — optional start/end, or mark it Permanent (always active, can't be deleted).
3
Wall sidebar preview — see exactly how the card renders at wall scale before saving.
4
Existing cards — each shows its schedule and how many flights currently match; toggle or delete.

Important (IMP)

Standing IMP limitations imported from the ops bulletin. A flight shows the "!" marker on the wall when it matches an entry's criteria — country, airport, operator or registration, respecting direction and date window, plus the flight type (commercial / private) and load (PAX / ferry). Auto-imported entries need a human review before they're trusted. Every entry is fully editable, takes file attachments, and carries added-by / confirmed-by audit stamps.

1
Needs-review filter — narrow to the auto-imported entries awaiting sign-off (65 after the reset).
2
Entry cards — status tags plus the criteria (ICAO / country) that trigger the match.
3
Detail pane — select an entry to read its verbatim body and match criteria.

12 · INTEGRATION

Webhooks · live updates

A webhook is simply Leon telling the wall the instant something happens, instead of the wall having to keep asking. This page is where you switch those live updates on and check they're working — you never need to touch Leon's own website.

BEFORE · POLLING

The wall asked Leon "what changed?" every 60 seconds. Leon never reported landings that way, so a flight that had landed could sit as flying on the wall for hours.

NOW · WEBHOOKS

Leon pushes an event the moment a flight lands, is cancelled, rescheduled or created — and the wall updates in about 2 seconds. No more stale "flying" pills.

Trusted, but verified. The wall doesn't blindly believe a pushed message — it re-checks that flight through its normal pipeline before changing anything. And the 60-second poll still runs underneath as a fallback, so if an event is ever missed, the next poll self-heals it.

Match flags — who gets it

Two things decide which flights a record surfaces on: where it applies (country and/or airport) and what kind of flight it applies to.

MATCH TYPE
Set Country only, Airport only, or both for a Mixed match. A flight matches if it touches any target you add. Country and airport pickers draw from the full airports directory.
APPLIES TO
All flights, Commercial only, or Private only — so a record can surface for scheduled charter but stay off private trips (or the reverse).
LOAD
All, PAX, or Ferry — a permit rule that only matters with passengers aboard stays off empty ferry legs, and vice versa.
