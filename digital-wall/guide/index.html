01 · ORIENTATION

The Digital Wall at a glance

The Digital Wall is your shared operational picture, split across two surfaces. Both read from the same live Leon feed, but you use them very differently.

SURFACE A · VIEW-ONLY

The wall (display / timeline)

The big dark screen in the ops room. A live timeline of every aircraft against the clock, with a status legend and manual limitations down the side. Nobody edits it — it reflects what the console decides.

Who uses it: everyone on shift, at a glance, from across the room.
SURFACE B · CONTROL

The console

The light-themed app you sign in to. Pick which flight is on the wall, run the NOTAM check, write limitations, manage aircraft and operators, tune settings, and request documents.

Who uses it: the dispatcher / ops user driving the shift.

02 · THE WALL

Reading the timeline

Each aircraft has its own horizontal lane. Flights appear as pills placed against the hour grid — left edge at departure, right edge at arrival. Everything you need to read a flight is on and around the pill.

What reaches the wall. Flights show from now out to the upcoming horizon (default 17 h) and drop off a set window after landing (post-landing removal, default 2 h) — both adjustable in Settings. Cancelled, crew-positioning and simulator flights are never shown.

Anatomy of a pill

A scheduled flight: the two airport ICAOs sit at each end of the pill, with the departure and arrival times underneath.

1
Flight ID — its colour signals the OPS checklist state and italic means an unconfirmed trip (see below).
2
Marker row — per-flight badges: IMP, WX, NTM and CAA (see markers).
3
ADEP → ADES — departure ICAO on the left, arrival ICAO on the right.
4
Times — departure under the left ICAO, arrival under the right. The pill spans exactly that window on the grid.

Pill fill = flight state

The pill's fill colour tells you where the flight is in its life. The sidebar legend is your reference — it's always on the wall.

The delay hatch & actual times

When a flight runs late, a diagonal hatch fills the gap between the planned and the actual time. As real times land, the pill relabels itself: ETD→ATD on departure and ETA→ATA on arrival.

1
Green flight ID — this trip's OPS checklist is complete. (Red would flag an outstanding OPS checklist.)
2
Delay hatch — the striped portion shows how much later than planned the flight actually ran.
3
Arrived fill — the dusty-mauve solid section confirms the flight is down.

The marker row

Above each pill, small badges flag anything that needs attention on that flight. They're generated automatically from the alert filter, the Important entries and matching CAA records. WX shows per airport, so a flight can carry a departure and an arrival badge at once.

The flight matches an Important entry — a standing operational limitation worth reading before you work it.
NTMNOTAM
A flagged NOTAM matched the alert filter for one of the flight's airports today — review it on the NOTAM Check page.

A weather category worth noting at an airport. It shows per airport — a small ↗ glyph for departure, ↘ for arrival — and takes the WX category colour (see §5).

The clock bar, hour spacing & auto-return

The clock bar across the top shows the home city and live time. Hour spacing controls how wide the hours are drawn — narrow fits more of the day on screen, wide spreads tight schedules apart. Both are set in the console's Settings.

HOUR SPACING 0.75× — MORE HOURS
HOUR SPACING 1.8× — WIDER GRID

03 · THE WALL

The flight side-overlay

When a dispatcher sends a flight to the wall from the console, a detail overlay slides in from the right. It's the full picture for the one flight that's live — and, like the rest of the wall, it's view-only.

You don't open it here — a dispatcher opens it from the console's Flights page with Show on wall. The banner names who opened it.

1
Header — the callsign, who opened it, and the trip reference.
2
Route & timings — ADEP → ADES with STD/ETD/ATD and STA/ETA/ATA, plus dep/arr delay.
3
Weather — decoded conditions for both airports, each tagged with its category (see §5).
4
Important (IMP) — the standing limitations this flight matches, in full.
5
Limitations — any manual limitation cards that match, further down the panel.
Reading, not doing. The overlay is there to be read from across the room. To change what's shown — swap the flight or close the overlay — go to the console's Flights page.

04 · DAILY WORKFLOW

The daily NOTAM check

Every morning the system runs a NOTAM scan at 10:00 Europe/Riga across every airport on today's flights, and the team reviews the result. Until every airport is signed off, the wall shows a red warning; once they're all done, it flips green.

1
Open NOTAM Check in the console's left nav.
2
Read each airport's flagged NOTAMs, using the keyword colours as a guide.
3
Press CHECKED per airport — your name and time are recorded.
4
When all airports are checked, the wall sign turns green.
THE PAGE IN ITS WARNING STATE
1
Red wall sign — while airports remain, Today’s wall sign shows CHECK NOTAM and the count still to review.
2
Progress short of complete — the bar and “10 of 11 airports checked · 1 remaining” track how far along the day is.
3
Mark checked — each unreviewed airport card carries the blue Mark checked button; press it once you’ve read the NOTAMs.
THE SAME PAGE, ALL AIRPORTS CHECKED
1
Today's wall sign — a live preview of what the wall is currently showing.
2
Run check now — re-runs the scan on demand (it also runs automatically at 10:00).
3
Progress & keyword legend — how many airports are done, and what each keyword colour means.
4
Per-airport CHECKED — sign off each airport; the name + time are stamped. Undo fixes mis-clicks.
5
Show all NOTAMs — expands from the flagged shortlist to the full, unfiltered list for that airport.

05 · REFERENCE

Weather (WX)

Each airport carries a flight category — a single colour-coded read of how good the conditions are. You'll see it as the WX badge on a pill, and decoded in full inside the side-overlay.

Weather on the wall is acknowledgment-only — it's there to inform, not something you action or clear.

VFR
Good — visual conditions.
MVFR
Marginal — reduced ceiling / vis.
IFR
Instrument conditions required.
LIFR
Low IFR — worst category.

06 · THE CONSOLE

Managing the console

Beyond Flights and NOTAM Check, four pages let you shape what the wall shows and how it behaves. Each carries its own "How this page works" panel at the top.

Operators

The Leon operators feeding the wall. Watch sync health at a glance, toggle an operator active/inactive, add a new one, or Force sync if the feed looks stale.

1
Sync health — data source, last run, cached flights, poll interval, last error.
2
Per-operator row — prefix, last sync, status, and an Active toggle.
3
Add operator — register a new Leon feed.

Aircraft

Choose which aircraft appear on the wall. Hiding an aircraft removes its lane from the timeline immediately. Search, filter by operator, or use Show all / Hide all.

1
On-wall count — how many aircraft are visible vs hidden, with bulk controls.
2
Upcoming counts — flights in the next 7 days and the next departure.
3
Show on wall — per-aircraft toggle; the lane appears or disappears at once.

Limitations REWORKED

Write the manual text limitations shown on the wall sidebar. Set the text, how it matches (flight, airport, country, or any mix — OR semantics), and its schedule. NOTAM, weather and IMP markers are generated automatically elsewhere.

1
Match type — Flight, Airport, Country or Mixed; matching is OR across every target you add. Country and airport pickers draw from the full airports directory.
2
Date window — optional start/end, or mark it Permanent (always active, can't be deleted).
3
Wall sidebar preview — see exactly how the card renders at wall scale before saving.
4
Existing cards — each shows its schedule and how many flights currently match; toggle or delete.

Important (IMP)

Standing IMP limitations imported from the ops bulletin. A flight shows the "!" marker on the wall when it matches an entry's criteria — country, airport, operator or registration, respecting direction and date window. Auto-imported entries need a human review before they're trusted. Every entry is fully editable, takes file attachments, and carries added-by / confirmed-by audit stamps.

1
Needs-review filter — narrow to the auto-imported entries awaiting sign-off (65 after the reset).
2
Entry cards — status tags plus the criteria (ICAO / country) that trigger the match.
3
Detail pane — select an entry to read its verbatim body and match criteria.
A REVIEWED ENTRY · EDIT, ATTACH & AUDIT
Fully editable
Correct the title, body, criteria and dates on any entry — imported or hand-written.
Attachments
Attach the source brief or permit so it travels with the entry.
Audit stamps
Every entry records who added it and who confirmed it, with timestamps.

Settings

Configure the wall's display and the NOTAM / alert filter that drives automatic flagging.

1
Display scale — global text/density; dial up until timings read from across the room.
2
Hour spacing — the gridline distance covered in §2 (0.5×–2.5×).
3
Wall clocks — add cities and drag to reorder; the home clock is highlighted on the wall.
4
NOTAM / alert filter — keyword groups (by category colour) that drive both the daily check and the NTM / WX badges.

07 · THE CONSOLE

CAA details

The CAA Details page holds Civil Aviation Authority contact details and permit processes — one record per authority. It replaces the shared OPS permit-contacts spreadsheet: when a flight matches a record, a teal CAA marker appears on its pill and the authority's contact block shows in the flight overlay.

Find it in the console's left nav, between Important and Settings.

How this page works
Contact details — the permit desk, phones, emails, AFTN/SITA and lead-time process for each authority. This replaces the shared CAA spreadsheet.
Match flags — an entry attaches to a flight by country and/or airport. The commercial / private flag narrows it so it only surfaces for that flight kind.
On the wall — matched flights get a teal CAA marker on the pill; the flight overlay shows the authority's contact block. Preview both inside the editor.

The authority list

The left column lists every authority. Search by name, country or ICAO, or filter by country, function and flight kind. Each row shows its match flags at a glance.

GB
Country code & flight kind — the two-letter country and whether it's Commercial only, Private only or All flights.
State dot — green when the record is active on the wall; grey when hidden (like DGAC France above).
fn
Function chip — what the authority covers (overflight, landing, slot coordination…).
AP
Match flags — country chips (purple) and ICAO airport chips (blue) show what the record matches.

A contact record

Selecting an authority opens its record. The contact fields mirror the old OPS permissions sheet column-for-column, so nothing from the spreadsheet is lost.

Product-accurate render · fields mirror the OPS permissions sheet: Country · Authority · Validity · Function · INFO · Contact · Phone · Mail · SITA · AFTN · VFR flight-plan addresses.

Match flags — who gets it

Two things decide which flights a record surfaces on: where it applies (country and/or airport) and what kind of flight it applies to.

MATCH TYPE
Set Country only, Airport only, or both for a Mixed match. A flight matches if it touches any target you add. Country and airport pickers draw from the full airports directory.
APPLIES TO
All flights, Commercial only, or Private only — so a record can surface for scheduled charter but stay off private trips (or the reverse).

How it surfaces on the wall

When a flight matches an active record, two things appear: a teal CAA marker on the pill, and the authority's contact block in the flight side-overlay. The editor previews both at wall scale before you save.

CAA
Teal marker — a matched flight carries the CAA badge alongside IMP, WX and NTM. It means an authority record applies to this flight.
Overlay block — the same overlay from §3 gains a teal-edged CAA section with the authority's validity, function, routing and channels.

08 · THE CONSOLE

AIP & GEN documents

Request briefing documents straight from a flight. Pick a flight on the Flights page, choose which airports and document types you need, and the pack is emailed to you — it is never downloaded to the wall.

FIG · REQUESTING FROM A FLIGHT
1

2

3
Send — the request queues and the pack is emailed to your inbox.
THE SEND CONTROLS · A REQUEST IN FLIGHT
1
Recipient — the pack is emailed to the signed-in user — never shown on the wall.
2
Departure / Arrival — choose which end(s) of the route you need documents for.
3
AIP / GEN / Both — pick the document type.
4
In flight — after Send, the request shows “Fetching documents (checking shared cache, then source)…”.
DELIVERED
1
Sent — the request confirms “AIP AD-2 · LSZH (departure) — Sent to …” once the email is on its way.
2
Send again — re-request the same pack in one click if you need it re-sent.
Delivery by email. Documents go to your inbox, never onto the wall — the wall stays a clean operational picture, and briefing packs stay with the person who needs them.
